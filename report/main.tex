\begin{abstract}
\noindent Models that ace training by exploiting \emph{spurious correlations} collapse
under distribution shift. On Colored-MNIST, color correlates with the label in training
($p_e \in \{0.1, 0.2\}$) and the correlation flips at test time ($p_e = 0.9$).
We compare plain empirical risk minimization (ERM) against two invariant objectives,
IRMv1 and V-REx, over 3 seeds with honest train-domain validation.
ERM latches onto color (train $0.994$ $\to$ OOD $0.315\pm0.010$).
IRMv1 recovers OOD accuracy only when the checkpoint is selected by peeking at the test
distribution ($0.799\pm0.030$ oracle) and collapses under honest selection
($0.243\pm0.172$); V-REx is the stable alternative ($0.424\pm0.014$ trainval,
$0.457\pm0.001$ at $\beta=10^4$).
An 18-case ablation program shows IRM is insensitive to $\lambda$ but bimodal in seed,
fails with a single environment, collapses to ERM level once a second spurious feature
appears, and transfers to Colored-FashionMNIST. Calibration, Integrated Gradients, and a
linear color-probe (decodability $1.00$ for both ERM and IRM) complete the diagnosis:
IRM down-weights color in the head rather than purging it from the representation.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} spurious correlation, invariant learning, IRM, risk
extrapolation, OOD generalization, model selection, trustworthy AI.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine learning models that achieve high in-distribution (ID) accuracy often collapse
under distribution shift when they rely on \emph{spurious correlations}: features that
correlate with the label in training environments but not at deployment.
A canonical illustration is ``cow on grass'': a classifier that detects grass instead of
the animal is accurate on a biased training set and wrong elsewhere.

This project studies this failure mode in a controlled setting, \textbf{Colored-MNIST}
\citep{arjovsky2019irm}: digit shape is the stable (causal) signal while color is
spuriously correlated with the label in training and anti-correlated at test time.
Our research questions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1 (reproduce):} does ERM latch onto color and collapse OOD, and do
invariant objectives recover OOD accuracy under honest model selection?
\item \textbf{RQ2 (break):} when does invariant learning itself fail (penalty strength,
backbone capacity, number of environments, second spurious feature, sample size, dataset)?
\item \textbf{RQ3 (diagnose):} which diagnostics show that a model abandoned color:
calibration, attributions, or representation probes?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contributions.}
(1) A mean$\pm$std comparison (3 seeds) of ERM / IRMv1 / V-REx under two model-selection
protocols, train-domain validation vs.\ OOD oracle \citep{domainbed2021}.
(2) An 18-case ablation program: IRM-$\lambda$-sweep, REx-$\beta$-sweep, MLP vs.\ CNN,
single-environment, second spurious patch, small-$n$, and Colored-FashionMNIST transfer.
(3) Reliability / Integrated-Gradients / linear-probe diagnostics linking the Reliability
and Explainability pillars of trustworthy AI.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM).}
\citet{arjovsky2019irm} learn a representation $\Phi$ such that a single classifier $w$
is simultaneously optimal in all training environments. The practical relaxation IRMv1
adds a gradient penalty (see \S\ref{sec:problem}). Follow-ups show IRMv1 is seed- and
$\lambda$-sensitive and propose games \citep{irmgames2020}, gradient-variance penalties
(Fishr, \cite{rame2022fishr}), and sparsity \citep{zhou2022sparseirm}.

\paragraph{Risk Extrapolation (REx) and Group DRO.}
V-REx \citep{krueger2021rex} penalizes the variance of risks across environments:
cheaper and empirically more stable than IRMv1. Group DRO \citep{sagawa2020dro} minimizes
the worst-environment risk; our codebase implements a GroupDRO-lite objective for
completeness and leaves its full sweep to future work.

\paragraph{When invariance fails.}
\citet{rosenfeld2021risks} prove IRM needs more environments than spurious features
($E > |\Psi_s|$); a single environment provably fails. \citet{zhou2022sparseirm} show
over-parameterization breaks IRM. \citet{domainbed2021} show model selection matters:
reported advantages shrink under train-domain validation, which we adopt as primary.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and last-layer findings.}
DomainBed \citep{domainbed2021}, WILDS \citep{koh2021wilds}, and SubpopBench
\citep{yang2023subpopbench} document benchmark disagreement: no method wins everywhere.
\citet{kirichenko2022dfr} (DFR) and \citet{liu2021jtt} (JTT) show core features are often
learned but mis-weighted in the head, motivating our linear color-probe. For
explainability we use Integrated Gradients \citep{sundararajan2017ig} as primary
evidence; vanilla saliency fails sanity checks \citep{adebayo2018sanity} and is kept as
baseline only.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\paragraph{Structural causal model.}
Let $D \in \{0,\dots,9\}$ be the digit, $\tilde Y = \mathbf{1}[D \ge 5]$,
$Y = \mathrm{flip}(\tilde Y; 0.25)$ the observed label, and $C$ the color id with
$C = \mathrm{flip}(Y; p_e)$. The image $X \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 28 \times 28}$ renders
the digit shape in red if $C=0$ else green. Shape $X \leftarrow D$ is stable; color
$X \leftarrow C$ is spurious and its correlation with $Y$ varies by environment $e$.

\paragraph{Input--output.}
Input $x$ (colored digit); output $\hat y \in \{0,1\}$ estimating $P(Y=1 \mid X)$.
Environments: $e_1: p_e=0.1$, $e_2: p_e=0.2$ (train), $e_{\mathrm{te}}: p_e=0.9$ (OOD test).
A color-only rule scores $\approx 0.85$ ID but $\approx 0.10$ OOD; a shape-only oracle
scores $\approx 0.75$ everywhere (label-noise ceiling).

\paragraph{Objectives.}
Let $R^e(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P_e}[\ell(f_\theta(x),y)]$ with binary
cross-entropy $\ell$. With per-env risks $R^e$:
\begin{align}
\text{ERM:} \quad & \min_\theta \tfrac{1}{E}\sum_e R^e(\theta). \\
\text{IRMv1:} \quad & \min_\Phi \sum_e R^e(w \circ \Phi)
  + \lambda \sum_e \big\|\nabla_{w=1} R^e(w \cdot \Phi)\big\|^2,
  \quad \lambda=0 \text{ for steps} < 190 \text{ (annealing).} \\
\text{V-REx:} \quad & \min_\theta \, \mathrm{mean}_e(R^e) + \beta \cdot \mathrm{Var}_e(R^e). \\
\text{GroupDRO-lite:} \quad & \min_\theta \max_e R^e(\theta).
\end{align}
The dummy-classifier trick ($w=1.0$) makes the IRM penalty first-order differentiable.

\paragraph{Metrics and model selection.}
For the test environment with groups $g=(y,c) \in \{0,1\}^2$:
ID-avg $= (\mathrm{Acc}_{e_1}+\mathrm{Acc}_{e_2})/2$;
OOD accuracy $= \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{te}}$;
OOD gap $=$ ID-avg $-$ OOD;
worst-group $= \min_g \mathrm{Acc}_g$ (a coarse equalized-odds proxy);
ECE with 15 bins.
Primary protocol is \textbf{train-domain validation} (checkpoint maximizing ID-avg);
the \textbf{OOD oracle} (maximizing test accuracy) is reported as reference to quantify
selection bias. All numbers are mean$\pm$std over 3 seeds unless stated otherwise.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Pipeline.}
\texttt{data}$\to$\texttt{train}$\to$\texttt{eval}$\to$\texttt{viz}:
\texttt{src/cmnist/data.py} builds Colored-MNIST/FashionMNIST (Arjovsky protocol +
optional $3\times3$ patch spurious); \texttt{models.py} gives an MLP ($2\times256$)
and a small CNN; \texttt{losses.py} implements ERM/IRMv1/V-REx/GroupDRO-lite;
\texttt{train.py} runs Adam ($lr=10^{-3}$, batch 2048, 2000 steps) with penalty annealing
and records best-ID / best-OOD checkpoints; \texttt{eval.py} reports per-env accuracy,
worst-group, gap, and ECE.

\paragraph{Experiment budget.}
\texttt{fast} (500 steps, 1 seed, pipeline demo), \texttt{base} (2000 steps $\times$
ERM/IRM/REx $\times$ 3 seeds, main comparison), \texttt{heavy} (penalty sweeps +
backbones + failure variants + FashionMNIST transfer, 54 runs total).
Total compute was $\approx$ 3\,h on an RTX 3080 (10\,GB); deterministic cuDNN,
seeds $\{0,1,2\}$. Below the annealing threshold (190 steps) every method degenerates
to ERM.

\subsection{Backbones, failures, and transfer (RQ2b)}
Table~\ref{tab:robust} stresses IRM along five axes. A second spurious patch drags IRM
($0.117\pm0.008$) down to ERM level ($0.146\pm0.002$), confirming the
$E > |\Psi_s|$ condition \citep{rosenfeld2021risks} in practice; a single environment
fails with extreme variance ($0.431\pm0.243$); small-$n$ widens variance further.
The effect transfers to Colored-FashionMNIST (IRM $0.650\pm0.063$ vs.\ ERM
$0.260\pm0.009$).
The backbone effect is protocol-dependent and we report it honestly: under trainval the
CNN averages higher OOD ($0.539\pm0.084$) than the MLP ($0.286\pm0.255$), but its
worst-group stays near $0.10$ --- capacity changes \emph{which} seed collapses, not
whether collapse happens.

the DFR observation \citep{kirichenko2022dfr} that core features are learned but
mis-weighted in the last layer.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ERM achieves near-perfect ID accuracy by exploiting color and collapses OOD
($0.994 \to 0.315\pm0.010$); IRMv1 recovers OOD accuracy only under OOD-peeking
selection ($0.799$) and collapses honestly ($0.243\pm0.172$), while V-REx is the stable
alternative at the same cost ($0.457\pm0.001$ at $\beta=10^4$).
Two environments do not suffice once a second spurious feature appears; one environment
fails outright; backbone capacity reshuffles but does not remove seed sensitivity.

\textbf{Lessons:} (1) always report train-domain validation alongside the OOD oracle ---
single-seed or oracle-only IRM numbers are not citable; (2) penalty strength matters less
than seed luck for IRMv1, while REx scales cleanly in $\beta$; (3) synthetic-only scope
(Waterbirds/CelebA excluded by budget) limits external validity; (4) spurious correlation
is a fairness issue (worst-group $\approx$ equalized odds): deploying on biased data
risks systematic harm to minority groups.
